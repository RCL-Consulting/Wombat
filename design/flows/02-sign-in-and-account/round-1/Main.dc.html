Wombat · Flow 02 · Sign-in and account · Round 1 wireframes · 2026-09-28

One structure: get in, be told plainly why not, keep your own account right

Greyscale boxes, real words, no colour or type choices. One proposal, with two options only where questions 1, 2 and 3 fork. A heavy left edge and a dark ! mark a refusal (danger); a light edge and an i mark a notice (info); a tick marks a success. A black outline is where the focus lands. Numbered black dots on a screen are explained on that row's notes board. Round 2 starts only after you confirm.

The questions

1 · What the sign-in page says about the product, and where the SSO buttons sit

One line of purpose under the lockup, "Work-based assessment for specialist training.", and no College or institution name. The sign-in page is one address for every institution and College on the platform; before sign-in Wombat cannot know which is the visitor's, so a name there would be wrong for most. An institution is named only on its own button, "Sign in with [institution]".

A · Institutions above the formRecommended

The buttons first, then "or sign in with your email and password", then the form. Someone whose hospital signs them in is not first offered a password field, where their hospital password fails and counts towards the lockout and the network throttle. When no provider is configured (today, everywhere) the page is the plain form. Boards 2 · A and 2 · 390.

B · Institutions below the form

The form first, then "or", then the buttons. Suits the people who must use a password: administrators ("An administrator account signs in with its password"), College staff and anyone without SSO. The refusal "…use its sign-in button below" reads the same in both, because the slot sits above both. Board 2 · B.

2 · Forgotten password

A · Say what to do, in plain wordsRecommended now

Replace the stub with how it works today (A.4.5): an administrator at your institution sets a new password and gives it to you; you sign in and choose your own. With SSO, a line that your institution resets that password. Nothing promised that is not built (BRIEF § 7 B10). The route and the link stay, so B can replace the page later. Boards 6 · A.

B · Reset by emailed link

Four screens: ask (email), check your email (the same words whether or not the address has an account, T287), choose a new password (from the link), link expired or used; then Sign in with a notice. Needs the PasswordResetEmail sent (flow 19), a single-use token endpoint and the throttle. Worth its own task with flow 19. Board 6 · B.

3 · Is a sign-out confirmation page wanted?

A · Only as the answer to a GETRecommended

The top bar's Sign out stays one press, a form post, as flow 01 built it. The Sign out page is what a typed or bookmarked GET /account/logout shows (T317), and /account/logout-confirm shows the same. A GET never signs out by itself: a link on another site could otherwise sign a person out. Afterwards the sign-in page says "You have signed out." (NEW), which matters on a shared ward computer.

B · Confirm every sign-out

The top bar's Sign out opens the page, and its button signs out: two presses every time, for every person, between patients. It guards only against a mis-press, whose cost is one sign-in that returns to the same page (returnUrl). It does not protect unsaved work; a form that holds work is that form's job, in its own flow.

4 · Should My account show the institution and institutional sign-ins, beside the roles?

Yes. The Account card shows the email once, the institution (for an account that has one) and the roles by label, one per line. A card "How you sign in" lists the password, with Change password, and each linked institutional sign-in, with Remove (T286; listing and removing links are not built). An account with no local password gets no Change password: the card says it signs in through its institution. Remove on the last way in is disabled, with its reason in words. Boards 4.
