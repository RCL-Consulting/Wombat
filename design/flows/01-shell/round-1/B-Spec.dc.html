bd

Several roles, and "switching"

  • No acting role. Dr Zulu sees Committee Reviews, Decision Panels and Activity Inbox together: 5 links (today 9 across two dashboards).
  • Every page names the role it serves in the eyebrow above its h1: "As Assessor". So the role shows on every page, set by the page, not by a mode.
  • Home holds a section per role in the brief's precedence. "Put Assessor first" reorders it; that is the whole switch. It changes the landing, never the navigation.
  • Held for the session, cleared at sign-out (T317).
  • This departs from the brief's goal wording ("they can switch role"): B replaces the switch with a view of both. Say if that is out of bounds.
c

Top bar

  • 56 px, sticky: the mark and Wombat, the tabs, then the name (link to My Account) and one Sign out.
  • The tabs never truncate. The name gives way first: truncated at 20 characters, whole in its accessible name and title.
  • A section's second row is 48 px, also sticky, so a section page loses 104 px of height to the frame (A loses 56).
f

States

  • As in A, in the one content column under the bar(s); the page footer stays.
  • Access denied and not found keep the top bar, with no tab lit and no second row.
  • The error bar spans the full width at the foot of the window.
g

The phone

  • The bar folds to the mark and a CSS-only Menu toggle. Folded, a 40 px strip under it says where you are ("You are in Home").
  • Open: every section expanded, its pages under its heading, then the name and Sign out.
  • The fold point is set by the longest bar (Administrator with a long name), about 1100 px; a 1024 px laptop gets the folded menu.

Edge cases

  • Long navs: no height problem; the Administrator's 17 links are 6 tabs and at most 4 in a second row.
  • A person with many roles (Speciality admin + Committee member + Assessor): 4 job links, still pages. The union of an Institutional admin with any review role is sections.
  • A role label that wraps: only in the eyebrow, which may take two lines.
  • Active state: one tab and, in a section, one second-row item. The second row is its own labelled nav ("Platform"), so a screen reader hears one current page.
